# Version History

| Version | Changes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Version 3.0.0** | Version 3.0.0 brings a new tectonic engine, new tectonic features to draw on your maps, more natural oceans with water you can see into and biomes on the seabed, relief shading, rivers that widen as they flow and meander across plains, and regional maps for Guided and Advanced (Tectonic) maps. Guided and Advanced (Tectonic) terrain now also looks the same at any map size, so you no longer need to raise Coastal Fracture on larger maps. The engine list now shows a version number next to Guided (Guided v2) and Advanced (Tectonics) (Advanced (Tectonics) v2), and every new map uses the latest version.<br><br>> Note: Maps you have already made are never changed without asking. They keep generating exactly as before until you choose to update them. When you open a map that the new terrain rules would change, a dialogue explains what would change and offers to update it. An update is not saved automatically: reload the map without saving to return to the original, or save it to keep the update. Updating a map made with the original tectonic engine builds completely new terrain, while everything you placed on the map (pins, routes, regions, labels, rivers and brush strokes) stays where it is, so only update a tectonic map you are happy to rework.<br><br>> Note: Some changes do reach existing maps. Every map uses the new way of drawing the terrain, sea and lakes, since it changes only how the map is drawn. As part of this, a frozen lake now shows Pack Ice, a land biome painted over a lake no longer shows on it, Pack Ice painted on land is no longer shown, and custom biomes set to render solid over water become Land and overwater biomes. Convergent and divergent fault lines now fade out smoothly at their edges instead of ending in a faint step, which relief shading showed as a line along both sides. Their shape changes only near their edges. Rivers are drawn the new way on every map (see Rivers below), but an existing map's rivers keep their routes and lakes until you update it.<br><br>**FEATURES**<br>- **Advanced (Tectonics) v2:** A new tectonic engine where the tectonic plates decide where the continents lie. Each plate is either buoyant continental crust or sinking ocean floor. Where plates collide they raise mountain ranges on land and trenches at sea; where they pull apart they open rift valleys on land and mid-ocean ridges at sea. Coastlines are shaped in the same way as Guided maps, with inlets, bays and small islands. Changing the number of plates reshapes the continents, but adding a single plate changes only a small part of the map. The original engine is now called Advanced (Tectonics) v1.<br>- **Regional maps from Guided v2 and Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps:** You can now create regional maps from Guided and Advanced (Tectonics) maps, as you already could from Standard and Flat maps. On a Guided map, use the new crop button in the land mask toolbar. On a tectonic map, use the "Create Regional Map from Existing Map" button. A Guided regional map receives all of its parent's land masks (including those outside the crop), so land that extends beyond the crop still generates as it does on the parent. A map on the original tectonic engine, or a Guided map made before this version, must be updated before you can crop it.<br>- **New coastline profile:** On Guided v2 and Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps, oceans are deeper. Continental Shelf now sets the width of a band of shallow sea along the coast, beyond which the seabed falls steeply to the deep ocean floor. A new Coastal Plains setting adds a band of low, gently rolling land along the coast before the land starts to rise. Both bands vary a little in width along the coast, so they do not trace the coastline exactly.<br>- **Ocean Scale:** A new setting for how quickly the seabed falls away beyond the Continental Shelf. Continent Scale now sets only how quickly the land rises.<br>- **Mid-Ocean Ridges:** Ridges now rise from the deep ocean floor, with a narrow rift valley along the crest. On Guided maps they run between continents, where the seabed is equally far from both (small islands do not create them). On Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps they form wherever plates pull apart under the sea. The crest wanders and kinks along its length, and abyssal hills (low hills running parallel to the ridge) cover its flanks and the ocean floor beyond them. Set Mid-Ocean Ridges to 0 for a flat ocean floor.<br>- **Relief shading:** The map now shades slopes as if lit from the north-west, so hills, valleys, ridges and seabed features stand out instead of blending into soft gradients. The shading is laid over the biomes too, so the shape of the land shows through them. The new Relief Shading setting ("Map Configuration" tool, "Visuals" section) sets its strength for each map, and 0 turns it off. A second setting, Seabed Relief, sets how much of the shading still shows through deep water.<br>- **Water:** The sea and lakes are now drawn as water you can see into. Shallow water is clear and leans turquoise, and it darkens with depth, so the seabed shows through near the coast and fades further out. Lakes are drawn in the same way. A new "Water" section in the "Map Configuration" tool sets the Water Tint (from blue-green through blue to blue-violet), Water Saturation and Water Clarity (how far you can see into the water) for each map.<br>- **Seabed biomes:** Biomes can now lie on the bed of the sea or a lake. Each custom biome has a new Placement setting (Land only, Underwater only, Overwater only, Land and underwater, or Land and overwater), which replaces the "Render solid over water" checkbox. Underwater biomes are seen through the water; overwater biomes sit on top of it, as Pack Ice does. Painting and auto-generation rules only place a biome where its placement allows, and it is never shown anywhere else. Deep Ocean and Shallow Ocean are now underwater biomes that can be painted like any other, and the biome brush's list now groups biomes by placement. The biome brush cannot yet paint underwater or overwater biomes onto lakes, but auto-generation rules and the default biomes do reach them.<br>- **Terrain and biome layers:** The base map, terrain and biomes buttons in the map controls are replaced by two buttons, Terrain Layers and Biome Layers. Each opens a list where you turn parts of the map on or off: Elevation, Relief Shading and Water for the terrain, and Land and Sea for the biomes (Land includes biomes that sit on the water, such as Pack Ice). A button is fully lit when all of its layers are on and partly lit when only some are. Every layer is on when a map opens.<br>- **Shoreline:** The coast of the sea is now outlined, dark on the land side and pale on the water side. Lakes have no outline, so rivers run into and out of them unbroken. The outline belongs to the contour layer, so the contour lines button shows and hides it, and it is drawn even when Contour Interval is 0.<br>- **Rivers:** Rivers are now drawn as water, in the same colour and tint as the sea and lakes, and frozen rivers as ice. A river starts narrow at its source and widens as it flows and as tributaries join it, narrowing where it falls steeply and widening where the land is flat. Large rivers widen at their mouth, and can split into a delta where they reach the sea across flat land. Rivers follow smooth curves rather than steps of pixels, and meander across flat plains. Their edges stay sharp however far you zoom in, and they are drawn the same way in the 3D view, custom rivers included. Rivers are part of the Water layer, so the Terrain Layers list shows and hides them with the sea and lakes, and the Toggle Features button is removed from the map controls.<br>- **Map updates:** Maps made before this version can be updated to the latest engines. The update dialogue opens when you load a map the update would change, and lists exactly what would change for that map. Tick "Don't ask again for this map" to stop it opening (this is saved straight away). You can still update the map later with the new "Update Map to latest engine(s)" button at the top of the "Create New Map" tool.<br>- **Tectonic features:** Fault lines are replaced by four tectonic features, each shaped by the terrain it crosses. A Mountain Range comes in three styles (Simple, Fold Belt and Rugged). A Subduction Zone cuts a trench on one side of its line and raises a chain of volcanoes on the other. A Rift opens a valley of tilted blocks with raised shoulders, and can have volcanoes of its own. A Hotspot Chain, modelled on Hawaii, builds volcanic islands that age along the chain from active volcanoes to eroded islands, atolls and seamounts, with settings for their spacing, scatter, variation and more. Symbols along each line show which way it runs, and a new Reversed checkbox in the edit dialogue turns it round. A feature can raise terrain above 100% elevation or lower it below 0% where the process calls for it. Every map made with this version uses the new features. Older maps keep the original fault lines until they are updated: convergent faults then become mountain ranges, divergent faults become rifts, hotspots become hotspot chains, and slip faults are removed.<br>- **Roughen brush:** A new terrain brush that gives the ground a fine, natural texture. Going over ground that is already rough changes nothing, the "Smooth Terrain" brush removes the texture, and Roughen puts it back. It always lays the same texture, so the Strength and Feather sliders are hidden while it is selected. The texture is laid out across the world rather than the map, so a roughened area looks the same on a regional map (with finer detail added), and it fades out towards the coast so it never moves the coastline.<br>- **Textured Flat maps:** A new "Textured ground" checkbox, shown in the "Create New Map" tool when the Flat engine is selected, gives a new Flat map the same fine texture as the Roughen brush instead of perfectly flat ground. It applies when you select "Create New Map", and Smooth and Roughen work on it as on any roughened ground.<br>- **Save As:** The toolbar now has a Save As button beside Save. Save As asks for a name and saves the map as a new saved map, which then becomes the open map, so a map you loaded can be changed and kept as a copy without overwriting the original. Save still saves over the open map (or asks for a name if the map has never been saved).<br><br>**IMPROVEMENTS**<br>- On new maps (and maps you update), rivers follow the lie of the land instead of running in long straight lines at fixed angles, and a river that runs beside another joins it rather than running alongside it.<br>- River Meander now sets how much rivers meander on flat land, from 0 (never) to 2 (also on gentler slopes). A map saved with the old River Meander keeps its value, turned into the new setting.<br>- New River Width setting (in Map Configuration, Hydrology) scales how wide every river is drawn.<br>- Custom rivers on new maps (and maps you update) are rivers like any other. The river runs exactly along the line you draw (from its higher end), stops where the line reaches the sea or another river, and otherwise carries on downhill from its end until it finds one. Procedural rivers that reach it join it, and it widens with them. The ground around the line is lowered into a gentle valley that keeps the texture of the land, instead of a channel cut into it. A custom river's Width sets how wide it is drawn where it starts.<br>- River sources on new maps (and maps you update) are placed afresh from the map's seed, land and climate every time the rivers are traced, instead of being saved as pins when the map is first made. They follow the land as you edit it, Spring Altitude and Spring Moisture take effect straight away, and a regional map finds the same river sources as its parent. While editing features, each procedural river source is shown as a red ring: delete it to remove it (a Removed River Source pin is added), or drag it to move it (it becomes a river source of your own). Updating an older map removes the river source pins it saved for its procedural rivers and places them afresh; river sources you added yourself are kept.<br>- Regional maps now keep the rivers that flow into them from outside. Each gets a River Inflow spring where it crosses the edge, so it enters as wide as it was on the parent map. Rivers on regional maps are drawn as wide as on the parent map, so they look wider the further you zoom in.<br>- Rivers no longer end at every small pool along their course: a river is drawn straight across a pool, or a narrow string of pools in a valley, and still stops at larger lakes. A lake that fills up (on new maps and maps you update) breaks out through the lowest ground around it and its river flows on, so rivers on flat ground no longer end in small pools or wander round the plain. A river only ends in a lake in a wide hollow with no outlet for a long way. A river also runs on through a small hollow below sea level inland instead of ending in it as if it had reached the sea, and one whose only way out of a hollow is off the edge of the map leaves the map there.<br>- The Max Lake Size setting is removed. The largest lake is now the same share of the world at any map size or zoom, so a regional map allows the same lakes as its parent. Maps made before this version keep the value they saved.<br>- On Guided v2 and Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps, Coastal Fracture, Continent Scale, Ocean Scale and the band widths are measured against a 1,000 pixel map and scaled to your map's size. The same settings now give the same world at any resolution, and larger maps add finer detail to their coastlines rather than smoothing them out. Updating a Guided map that is not 1,000 pixels across lowers its Coastal Fracture to keep its coastline close to its current shape.<br>- Continent Scale no longer moves the coastline. Coastal Fracture is now the main control over the shape of the coast.<br>- Small islands now rise to hills with high points of their own instead of staying almost flat, while large continents keep the broad slopes Continent Scale gives them.<br>- Regional maps now gain finer detail the further you zoom in. Terrain (except on Flat maps), moisture and temperature all add detail, so coastlines and biome borders become more intricate while the overall shape of the world stays the same. Existing regional maps are offered this as an update.<br>- On Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps, a mountain range raised where plates collide can now climb above 100% elevation (and a trench can fall below 0%) instead of being flattened into a plateau at the limit. Most terrain still generates between 0% and 100%.<br>- Every slider in the side panel now shows its name, an information icon and its current value on one line, above a full-width slider. On Guided v2 and Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps, the coastline settings are grouped under Land and Sea headings.<br>- The Contour Interval slider now redraws the map once you stop moving it, rather than at every step, which made it slow to respond on large maps. Relief Shading, Seabed Relief and the water settings change the map straight away without redrawing it.<br>- A map's journal page in the map compendium now also lists the engine's version, a regional map's magnification, Textured ground on Flat maps, the Coastal Plains, Continent Scale, Ocean Scale and Mid-Ocean Ridges settings of Guided v2 and Advanced (Tectonics) v2 maps, Relief Shading, Seabed Relief, Region Opacity and the water settings, each biome's placement, and a count of each kind of feature on the map (custom rivers, river sources added and removed, tectonic features, points of interest, routes, region layers, regions, labels and decorations). Scale settings now show the value their slider shows (for example 250 rather than 0.004), and every piece of text on the page can be translated. A map's page is rewritten the next time you save it.<br>- The 3D view now matches the map. The seabed shows its biomes under water coloured by depth and tinted as on the map, lakes lie flat at their surface, Pack Ice sits on top of the sea, and layers you have turned off stay off. Rivers are painted onto the ground instead of floating above it as thin lines, and they stay visible with the whole map in view.<br>- The "Smooth Terrain" brush now pulls the ground towards the average of the ground around it. Before, it moved each pixel only a tiny step towards the pixel under the brush centre, so it barely changed anything even at full Strength. Strength and Feather still set how much and how softly it smooths. Like Roughen, it never moves the coastline, so land stays land and sea stays sea. Brush strokes painted before this version replay exactly as they were.<br>- The "Level Terrain" brush now leaves the ground it levels with the same fine texture as the Roughen brush, so levelled ground looks flat rather than featureless. Use "Smooth Terrain" afterwards for perfectly smooth ground. Level strokes painted before this version replay exactly as they were.<br>- Fault lines can now be up to 400 pixels wide with the toolbar slider, and up to 1,000 in the fault's edit dialogue, since a fault widened on a regional map (see below) can be wider than the old limit of 100.<br>- The Edit Fault Line dialogue now sets Thickness and Strength with sliders, as the toolbar does, and shows only the settings for the chosen type.<br>- Points of interest no longer show their name and description in a tooltip when you hover over them, which could stay on screen after the pointer had moved on (for example, while painting over them). Descriptions are still included in the Scene Journal when you export a map to a scene.<br>- Route line thickness is now set with a slider in the route edit, mass edit and quick style dialogues, matching the toolbar.<br>- The lists in the Create New Map (land masks), Features, Infrastructure, Regions, Labels and Cartography tools can now be collapsed by clicking their heading, and each region layer can be collapsed to its header. Collapsed lists give their space to the open ones. Lists stay as you leave them while the Map Builder is open.<br><br>**BUG FIXES**<br>- Rain shadows on regional maps now match their parent map. The wind's reach was scaled by the zoom twice, and also depended on the shape of the crop, so rain shadows (and the forests, grasslands and deserts they shape) were placed too far downwind. Existing regional maps are offered this fix as an update.<br>- Fault lines and hotspot chains on regional maps now look as they do on the parent map. Their width stayed the same number of pixels when a regional map was created, so on a x4 regional map a fault was a quarter as wide, and a hotspot chain had four times as many, smaller volcanoes; their texture was also drawn at a different scale. A fault that crossed the cropped area without one of its points lying inside it was also left out. Existing regional maps are offered this fix as an update.<br>- Convergent and divergent fault lines no longer end in a faint step at their edges (see the note above).<br>- The Level Terrain, Slope Up and Slope Down brushes now work on a regional map when a stroke starts outside the cropped area. These strokes take their height from the ground where they start, which a regional map did not have, so they did nothing there. A regional map now uses the height the stroke started at on the parent map.<br>- Slope Up and Slope Down strokes on a regional map now climb and fall as steeply as on the parent map. They climbed faster the further you zoomed in (four times as steeply on a x4 regional map).<br>- Create an existing regional map again to apply these two fixes to it.<br>- Terrain and biome brushes no longer lag behind the mouse on large maps. Moving the mouse a short distance with a brush redrew the whole map, which on a large map could take most of a second each time, so a slow stroke or a large brush could barely keep up. Painting also no longer redraws the map's pins, routes, rivers, regions and labels with every mouse move, which on a map with many of them used up memory until the map stopped drawing.<br>- Working quickly on a large map no longer leaves the map studio busy for minutes. When a stroke's refresh took longer than the pause before the next one began, the refreshes ran side by side, repeating each other's work, and saving or closing the window had to wait for all of them. Refreshes now run one at a time, and any asked for while one is running are combined into one.<br>- Closing the map studio while it is refreshing or saving no longer shows errors in the console. Closing now waits for a save that is under way to finish.<br>- Region Opacity is now saved with the map. It went back to 0.5 whenever a map was loaded.<br>- Two rivers no longer run side by side in straight parallel lines (on new maps and maps you update).<br>- Procedural rivers now reach the sea and join the river they flow into. Their last step was left out, leaving a small gap.<br>- After exporting a map to a scene, the terrain, contour and feature layers return to how you had them. They were all turned back on.<br>- The region list could run past the bottom of the Regions tool when a layer held many regions, hiding the rest of the list and the other layers. The layers now scroll together in one list, with the Edit Regions button always in view.<br>- A map size typed into Map Resolution is no longer lost when the seed is randomised (or anything else redraws the panel) before Create New Map. Typing a new size also no longer marks the open map as having unsaved changes.<br>- Choosing a quick style in a dialogue now updates the filled part of the font size slider as well as its number.<br>- A name containing quotation marks is no longer cut short in the name prompts (such as Save As or renaming a region layer).<br><br>**UNDER THE HOOD**<br>- Maps now store a `terrainVersion` (1 for maps saved before this version, 2 for new maps and every regional map created from now on). Version 2 also selects the current river rules (`TerrainVersion.usesCurrentRivers`, `params.terrain.currentRivers`). The new `TerrainVersion` module resolves it into the plain values the engines read from `params.terrain` (resolution scale, extra and detail octaves, whether the current coastal profile applies, and a `world` frame of zoom, origin and top map size), so the engines never read the version themselves and version 1 maps generate bit-identically to v2.6.0.<br>- New `TerrainUpgrade` works out what an update would change (for regional maps, by recomputing the climate with the corrected settings and comparing biomes, stopping at the first difference), applies it, and writes the "don't ask again" choice to the saved journal as a targeted update of that one field, leaving unsaved edits untouched.<br>- Guided terrain is now generated in the pixels of the top map (the map every regional map was ultimately cropped from). The coast distance field is built with a jump flood over the map's footprint plus a margin, at up to one cell per regional pixel (capped by `GENERATION.COAST_FIELD_MAX_CELLS`), so a regional map sees the same coastlines as its parent even when none lies inside the crop.<br>- New `ProceduralEngine#generateTectonicV2Topography` builds a plate model with its own random stream, decides land from continent noise plus plate buoyancy (which leans towards the land or sea already under each plate, so one more plate changes little), and then shares the guided coast field and detail pass. The original `generateTectonicTopography` is unchanged and still used for version 1 tectonic maps.<br>- The current coastal profile (`#shapeCoastalProfile`, `#shapeLand` and `#shapeSeabed`) is configured by `GENERATION.COASTAL_PROFILE`. Landmass sizes come from `#measureLandmasses`; mid-ocean ridges come from a coarse ridge field over the whole top map (`#buildRidgeField` for Guided, `#buildPlateRidgeField` for tectonic maps) shaped by `#ridgeLift`. On this profile the detail noise is read at each pixel's own position rather than the warped one (the warp stretched the finer octaves into streaks that relief shading made obvious), and output is no longer clamped to the 0 to 1 range.<br>- `RegionalExtractor` records a compounding `world` frame, carries every land mask into Guided regional maps, and sets the wind distance from the top map's shape instead of multiplying it by the zoom. `#fbm` accepts extra octaves, weighted so the base octaves keep their shape. The climate pass is split into `prepareClimate`, `getMoistureAt` and `getTemperatureAt`, which `TerrainUpgrade` reuses.<br>- The terrain is now drawn on the GPU. New `TerrainCompositor` (a PIXI `Mesh` with a custom shader) mixes three map-sized images: the land and surface biomes, the underwater biomes, and a packed image of relief, water depth and land height written by the new `ProceduralEngine.paintTerrainAux`. `TerrainShading` holds the shader and an identical CPU copy (`shade`) used to check it. The layer switches, biome opacity, relief strength and water settings are shader uniforms, so changing them never repaints. `colorize`, `createBaseMap` and their ocean, lake and land painters are removed, and the base, topography and biome canvas layers are replaced by one `terrain` layer, keeping the same number of map-sized images. Sea depth is measured against the map's deepest point, and lake depth from the lake's surface (scaled by `WATER.LAKE_DEPTH_SCALE`). The look is configured by `DISPLAY.TERRAIN`, `DISPLAY.WATER` and `DISPLAY.SHORELINE`, and maps now save `seabedRelief`, `waterClarity`, `waterHue` and `waterSaturation` in `params.display`.<br>- Relief (`ProceduralEngine#reliefChange`, configured by `DISPLAY.RELIEF`) is measured per pixel of a 1000 pixel map, so the same hill is shaded the same at any map size or regional zoom, and flat ground keeps exactly its unshaded colour.<br>- New `BiomePlacement` resolves each biome's placement into sides (land, underwater and overwater, as bit flags). `BiomeRuleEngine.matchBiomeId` skips rules on a side their biome may not appear on, and `ProceduralEngine.resolveBiomeLookup` resolves a land biome, or an underwater and an overwater biome for water (the sea or a lake), each by override, rule and default. `params.biomeSides` replaces `params.solidOverWater`, and custom biomes save a `placement` (older saves with `solidOverWater` are still read). New biome strokes record the sides they may paint (`paintSides`); strokes without it replay exactly as before.<br>- `createContourMap` takes the water mask and draws the shoreline after the contour lines.<br>- `Scene3D.render3DMap` is built from the map's own images and settings. Its drape comes from `TerrainShading.drape` (the shader's steps without relief, worked out once per depth and height value), rivers come from the same river image the flat map draws (see below), the texture is mipmapped with anisotropic filtering, and the `THREE.Line` rivers are removed. The 3D settings are in `DISPLAY.THREE_D`.<br>- Rivers are drawn by the terrain shader. New `RiverNetwork` turns the traced rivers into drawn channels: flow (length of river upstream, in pixels of a 1,000 pixel map) added up along each river and from each tributary at the step it joins; a half-width per point from the flow, the slope and River Width; Gaussian-smoothed centrelines with a gentle wander and Kinoshita-curve meanders steered along the smoothed line (calmed where a bend would reach water or climb); tributaries ending on the bent line of the river they join; and delta branches traced from a fork towards the sea. `RiverNetwork.rasterise` paints a fourth terrain image, per pixel a distance field to the nearest river's edge, a frozen share, and pools (lakes too small or narrow to stop a river, which a river is drawn across). The shader turns the distance field into a river edge softened over one screen pixel, which `TerrainCompositor`'s mesh works out from its world transform before each draw. On a refresh, only the channels that changed (and where the water changed) are repainted and uploaded. `StudioCanvas` no longer draws procedural rivers as `PIXI.Graphics`, so vector repaints no longer rebuild every river, and `RiverSteps` and the `DISPLAY.THREE_D` river settings are removed.<br>- `ProceduralEngine#traceRiver` records which river and step each river joins (a per-pixel owner buffer). On current maps it also joins a river it runs beside (`#findAdjacentRiver`) and, going downhill, steps to the lower neighbour closest to a point carried straight down the slope (`#followSlope`); the legacy River Meander noise applies only to legacy maps. On current maps, custom rivers are traced before any spring: `HydrologyEngine.authoredRivers` turns each spline into a path of pixels with an inflow from its Width, `ProceduralEngine#traceAuthored` follows it (ending at the sea or joining a river, otherwise tracing on from its end), and each river records how many of its steps are `authored`, which `RiverNetwork` keeps on the line (no wander or meander, easing in beyond it). `HydrologyEngine.carveManualRivers` shapes their valley (`#carveValley`): the most any point of the line lowers each pixel, falling off with a cosine over `MANUAL_RIVER_VALLEY_SPREAD` times the river's radius, subtracted from the ground so its texture survives. Legacy maps keep the trench and the spring at the line's top (`getRiverSources`). `RegionalExtractor` adds a spring with an `inflow` for every river entering the crop, and spring pins outside the map are no longer traced. On current maps `#fillBasin` floods a river's own earlier pixels instead of skipping them, limits a lake to `HYDROLOGY.MAX_LAKE_AREA` square baseline pixels (`LOOP_LAKE_AREA` when it floods its own river), and a full lake is breached (`#breachBasin`): the flood's search carries on past the lake, without adding to it, until it reaches ground lower than the lake's surface, and the river cuts the channel the search took to get there. The search covers up to `HYDROLOGY.BREACH_AREA` square baseline pixels, and reaching the map's edge lets the river out there. `#isSea` tells open sea from an inland hollow below sea level (a group of such pixels no larger than the largest lake, not reaching the map's edge), which a river runs through like a lake. A river hemmed in by its own course pools and breaches the same way. New `RiverSources` places the procedural springs on current maps from `RIVER_DENSITY` × `SPRING_CANDIDATES` candidate places drawn in the world (so a regional map shares its parent's), keeps those on land `springAltOffset` above sea level and wetter than `springMoistMin`, and drops any a `block_spring` pin sits on. Nothing is stored for them: `app.proceduralSprings` holds the current ones for the canvas, which draws them (`DISPLAY.PROCEDURAL_SPRING`) as targets the user can delete or drag. `RiverSources.findBakedPins` recognises the pins the legacy bake made by replaying its sequence, and `ProceduralOrchestrator` removes them when a map first uses the current rules. `createContourMap` no longer takes the water mask: the shoreline is drawn at the coast only. `TerrainUpgrade` reports river changes (`changesRivers`, `changesChannels`) and has a "rivers" kind for legacy maps whose only change is their rivers.<br>- New `templates/parts/shared-range.hbs` partial and `RangeDisplay` helper keep each slider's value display and fill in step, wired once through a document `input` listener and a `renderApplicationV2` hook.
